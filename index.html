    Posture Resets

    Small adjustments that unload your lumbar spine across the day.

    Sitting

    Hips slightly higher than knees. Feet flat. Screen at eye level. Shoulders back and down.

    Standing

    Soft knees, weight even on both feet, ribs stacked over pelvis. Shift your weight every few minutes.

    Sleeping

    Side sleeping with a pillow between the knees, or on your back with a pillow under the knees.

    Lifting

    Hinge at the hips, keep the object close, bend the knees — let your legs do the work, not your back.

    Daily Habits That Help

    • Move every 30 minutes. A one-minute walk resets pressure in the discs.
    • Walk 20–30 minutes a day. Low-impact activity is one of the best-studied treatments.
    • Hydrate. Intervertebral discs are mostly water.
    • Strengthen your core gradually. Think glute bridges and dead bugs, not sit-ups.
    • Manage stress. Tension amplifies pain perception — try 4-7-8 breathing.
    • Heat, then move. 10 minutes of heat followed by gentle movement often beats rest alone.

    When to See a Doctor

    This site is educational and is not a substitute for medical care.

    • Pain after a fall, crash, or significant trauma
    • Numbness, tingling, or weakness in a leg or foot
    • Loss of bladder or bowel control — seek emergency care
    • Fever, unexplained weight loss, or night sweats with back pain
    • Pain that wakes you at night or keeps worsening over weeks

    Made with care. Always listen to your body and consult a qualified clinician for persistent pain.
